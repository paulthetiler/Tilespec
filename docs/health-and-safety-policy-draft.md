# TileSpec — Health & Safety Policy (DRAFT)
Version: 9 November 2026. Internal working draft. Adapted in structure and topic coverage from the 2020 Commercial Tiling Solutions Ltd policy supplied for reference. This is a NEW TileSpec draft, not an adopted or signed policy. Accountable personnel, resources, procedures and legal compliance must be confirmed before issue.

## 1. Statement of intent
TileSpec intends to prevent injury and work-related ill health to workers, subcontractors, clients, visitors and members of the public affected by its activities. We will assess significant risks, plan and supervise work, provide safe equipment and suitable controls, consult workers and improve arrangements following incidents, inspections and changes in operations. Safety takes priority over programme or commercial pressure. This policy is subject to approval and signature by TileSpec's responsible director.

## 2. Organisation and responsibilities
The TileSpec director retains overall accountability for health and safety, provision of adequate resources, appointment of competent assistance and annual policy review. A named person must be appointed for risk assessments, COSHH, training, inspections, incident reporting, emergency planning and records. A nominated site supervisor will coordinate daily safe work, check inductions and RAMS, inspect working areas, stop unsafe activity and escalate concerns. Workers and subcontractors must take reasonable care, follow safe systems, use PPE, report hazards and cooperate with supervision. Responsibility matrix and named appointments: TO BE CONFIRMED.

## 3. Risk assessments and method statements
Before each project, review the site, scope, interfaces, hazards, client requirements and construction phase plan. Prepare and brief suitable site-specific risk assessments and method statements (RAMS), including emergency arrangements and work sequencing. Review RAMS when design, access, materials, plant, workforce or conditions change. Keep signed or otherwise verifiable briefing records. Separate RAMS are required for unusual or high-risk operations.

## 4. Site inductions, competence and subcontractors
Check trade competence, training, relevant CSCS or equivalent evidence where required, supervision needs and suitability of subcontractors before site access. Deliver a TileSpec task briefing and comply with the principal contractor's site induction and rules. Ensure instructions are understood, including where language or literacy support is required. Maintain training and induction records. No operative is authorised to use equipment or undertake specialist tasks without suitable competence.

## 5. Construction design and management
Determine TileSpec's actual role on each project under the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015. Cooperate with the client, principal contractor, principal designer and other trades; provide relevant information, follow the construction phase plan and coordinate activities. Do not assume duties allocated to another dutyholder. Clarify welfare, access, shared areas and site emergency arrangements before starting.

## 6. Manual handling and large-format tiling
Assess handling of tiles, slabs, boards, adhesives, equipment and waste. Plan delivery, unloading, storage and movement routes. Use suitable mechanical aids, lifting frames, trolleys, suction equipment and sufficient trained operatives for heavy or awkward loads. Verify equipment capacity and substrate/support conditions. Avoid unsupported manual handling and prevent crush injuries, falling slabs and sharp-edge cuts.

## 7. Dust, silica and respiratory protection
Assess exposure to respirable crystalline silica from cutting, drilling, grinding and chasing tiles or substrates. Prefer suitable wet-cutting or effective on-tool extraction with appropriate filtration; avoid uncontrolled dry cutting and sweeping. Where residual exposure requires RPE, select suitable protection, provide training, face-fit testing for tight-fitting masks and maintenance. Consider other workers and the public. Review applicable exposure controls and health surveillance needs with competent advice.

## 8. COSHH and hazardous materials
Maintain current safety data sheets and task-specific COSHH assessments for cement-based adhesives and grout, primers, levelling compounds, resins, solvents, sealers and cleaning chemicals. Address skin burns, dermatitis, eye damage, inhalation and ventilation. Specify suitable gloves, eye protection, washing facilities, storage, spill procedures and disposal. Brief operatives before use and do not substitute products without reassessment.

## 9. Tools, electricity, noise and vibration
Inspect saws, mixers, grinders, drills, extension leads, guards and dust controls before use; remove defective equipment from service. Maintain inspection and servicing records proportionate to risk. Control electrical risks including wet working, cable routing and appropriate RCD protection. Assess noise and hand-arm vibration, select lower-exposure methods, limit exposure and arrange relevant health surveillance where required.

## 10. Work at height, access and falling objects
Avoid work at height where reasonably practicable. Select and inspect appropriate platforms, towers or other access equipment and ensure competent erection and use. Ladders are for appropriate low-risk short-duration tasks only. Prevent falling materials, protect people below and coordinate exclusion zones. Do not improvise access arrangements.

## 11. Slips, trips, housekeeping and protection
Maintain clear routes, tidy cables, manage wet floors and slurry, store materials securely and segregate waste. Coordinate floor protection with the main contractor and other trades. Keep fire exits, access routes and emergency equipment unobstructed. Waste handling must follow site arrangements and applicable environmental requirements.

## 12. Asbestos and existing building hazards
Before intrusive refurbishment, obtain and review relevant asbestos information and surveys where required. Stop work immediately if suspect material is encountered, prevent disturbance and notify the responsible site party. TileSpec will not undertake asbestos removal or disturbance unless separately authorised, appropriately competent and compliant with applicable requirements. Assess other hidden hazards before drilling, chasing or removal.

## 13. Fire, first aid, welfare and emergencies
Confirm site fire alarms, evacuation routes, assembly points, first-aid arrangements, welfare and emergency contacts before work. Obtain permits and appropriate controls for any hot works; follow site-specific fire-watch requirements. Provide adequate drinking water, toilets and washing arrangements through the responsible dutyholder. Workers may stop and withdraw from serious or imminent danger and must report it promptly.

## 14. Incidents, near misses and RIDDOR
Report all injuries, near misses, dangerous occurrences and unsafe conditions promptly to the site supervisor and responsible office contact. Make the area safe, preserve relevant evidence where appropriate and investigate causes and corrective actions. The appointed competent person will determine whether statutory RIDDOR reporting applies and meet required reporting timescales. Keep incident records securely.

## 15. Health, wellbeing and fitness for work
Assess risks from fatigue, heat, cold, repetitive work, kneeling, vibration, noise and exposure to substances. Make reasonable arrangements for health surveillance where indicated by risk assessment. Workers must not work impaired by alcohol, drugs or medication affecting safe performance; concerns are managed confidentially and proportionately.

## 16. Consultation, monitoring and records
Encourage workers to raise concerns without penalty. Supervisors will record inspections, corrective actions, RAMS briefings, equipment checks and incidents. Management will periodically audit sites, review performance and close corrective actions. Maintain controlled versions of policies, RAMS, COSHH, training and inspection records in the business document system with appropriate access.

## 17. Review, approval and implementation
Review at least annually and after significant legal, organisational, equipment or operational changes, or serious incidents. Before adoption, confirm the legal trading entity, responsible director, competent adviser, actual supervisor arrangements, insurances, training provision, site records, emergency arrangements and all referenced procedures. This draft must not be represented as evidence that controls are already implemented.
